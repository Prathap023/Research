UNIT I – RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

01

Introduction to Research

Meaning & Definition Research is a systematic and scientific process used to solve problems and discover new knowledge.

It involves:

  • Identifying a problem
  • Formulating a hypothesis
  • Collecting data
  • Analyzing data
  • Drawing conclusions
Standard Definitions “Research is a careful investigation or inquiry specially through search for new facts in any branch of knowledge.”

“Research is a systematized effort to gain new knowledge.” – Redman and Mory
02 & 03

Objectives & Motivation

Objectives:

  • Find hidden truths and new knowledge
  • Gain familiarity with a phenomenon
  • Achieve new insights
  • Describe characteristics of individuals or groups accurately
  • Determine frequency of occurrence
  • Identify relationships between variables
  • Test hypotheses

Reasons for Doing Research (Motivation):

  • To earn a research degree
  • To solve unsolved problems
  • Intellectual joy of creative work
  • Service to society
  • Gain recognition and respect
04

Types of Research (A-D)

A. Descriptive

Research that describes the present state of affairs.
  • Uses surveys and fact-finding enquiries.
  • Researcher has no control over variables.
  • Reports what is happening.

B. Analytical

Research that analyzes existing information critically.
  • Uses already available facts/data.
  • Makes critical evaluation.

C. Applied

Research aimed at solving practical problems (e.g., Marketing or Evaluation research).

D. Fundamental

Research focused on formulation of theories and generalizations (Knowledge for knowledge’s sake; Pure/basic research).
04

Types of Research (E-H)

E. Quantitative

Research based on measurement of quantity or amount. Expressed in numerical form.

F. Qualitative

Research related to qualitative phenomena such as opinions and feelings (e.g., Motivation research).

G. Conceptual

Research related to abstract ideas or theories. Used to develop new concepts or reinterpret existing ones.

H. Empirical

Data-based research verified through observation or experiment. Supports/disproves hypothesis based on experience.
05

Research Approaches

A. Quantitative Approach

  • Inferential: Uses sample data to infer characteristics of population (Example: Survey research).
  • Experimental: Manipulates variables to observe effects (Greater control over research environment).
  • Simulation: Construction of artificial environment for generating data.

B. Qualitative Approach

Subjective assessment of attitudes, opinions, and behavior. Not subjected to rigorous quantitative analysis.
06 & 07

Significance, Methods & Methodology

Significance: Helps obtain higher degrees, source of livelihood, outlet for new ideas, develops creative work, and helps formulate new theories.

Research Methods Techniques used for conducting research. Groups include: Data collection methods, Statistical techniques, Evaluation techniques.
Research Methodology Science of understanding how research is done. Includes the logic behind methods and why a particular method is used.
08 & 09

Scientific Method & Process

Research is a systematic and scientific search for knowledge. Scientific method is the logical and organized way of doing research.

Features of Scientific Method: Systematic, Logical, Empirical, Controlled, Objective, Replicable.

Research Process Steps:

  1. Identify problem
  2. Literature review
  3. Hypothesis formulation
  4. Research design
  5. Data collection
  6. Data analysis
  7. Conclusion and report writing
10 & 11

Criteria & Problems in India

Criteria of Good Research:

  • Purpose clearly defined & uses common concepts
  • Procedure explained clearly
  • Results objective & honest reporting (Acknowledges flaws)
  • Appropriate statistical tests & reliable outcome measures
  • Conclusions justified with data
  • Logical and systematic

Problems Encountered in India:

  • Lack of scientific training & insufficient interaction
  • Lack of confidence among participants
  • Overlapping research studies
  • Inadequate library management
  • Difficulty in obtaining published data
  • Problem of conceptualization

DEFINING THE RESEARCH PROBLEM

01 - 04

Defining the Problem

What is a Research Problem? A question or issue that needs to be solved through research.

Selecting the Problem

A Good Problem Should Be: Interesting, Feasible, Useful, and Not too broad or narrow.

Bad Example: Study cloud computing
Good Example: Improve security in hybrid cloud using AI

Necessity of Defining

  • Gives direction to research
  • Helps identify required data and select techniques
  • Avoids confusion

Technique Involved

  1. Statement of problem in general way
  2. Understanding nature of problem
  3. Surveying literature
  4. Developing ideas through discussions
  5. Rephrasing problem into working proposition

UNIT II – LITERATURE REVIEW

01 & 02

Literature Review & Sources

Literature Review A collection of research publications, books, and documents related to the problem. Purpose is to avoid repeating work, understand current progress, learn techniques, and identify gaps.

Primary Sources

Data collected directly by researcher (e.g., Surveys, Interviews, Experiments, Observations).

Secondary Sources

Data collected by someone else and already available (e.g., Books, Research papers, Websites, Reports).
03 - 06

Reviews, Monographs & Patents

3. Review

Summarizes and analyzes many research studies on a topic. No new experiments, combines multiple studies, explains trends/gaps.

4. Monograph

A detailed book/report on a single topic. Focuses on one topic, more detailed than textbooks.

5. Patents

A legal document giving exclusive rights for an invention. Shows innovation, contains technical details, protected by law. Used to avoid copying and understand latest technologies.

6. Research Databases

Includes Journal databases, Reference databases, Online repositories.

07 & 08

Web as a Source & Searching

Uses: Access journals/books, collect data, publish research, communication.

Online Tools: E-mail, E-journals, Video conferencing, Online questionnaires.

Pros vs Cons:

  • Advantages: Saves time, Low cost, Fast communication, Wide reach.
  • Challenges: Reliability issues, Ethical concerns, Information overload.

Searching Activities: Searching journal articles, finding conference details, finding databases, collecting reference materials.

09 - 11

Critical Review & Gaps

9. Critical Literature Review Evaluating previous studies critically. Includes summarizing, evaluating strengths/weaknesses, comparing, and identifying gaps.
10. Identifying Gap Areas Finding missing or less-studied areas. Identified by reading future work sections, studying limitations, finding missing data, and conflicting results.
11. Working Hypothesis A prediction made before starting research. Keeps research focused and defines the area. Developed via expert discussions, record examination, similar studies review, and small interviews. Must be clear, testable, and properly written.

RESEARCH DESIGN

01 - 03

Meaning, Need & Features

1. Meaning of Research Design The blueprint or overall plan of research. Specifies what data to collect, from where, how to collect, and how to analyze.

2. Need for Research Design

  • Provides direction
  • Efficient use of resources
  • Minimizes errors
  • Improves reliability and validity

3. Features of Good Design

  • Clear objective, Simplicity, Flexibility
  • Accuracy, Reliability, Validity
  • Economy, Minimizes bias
04 & 05

Components & Types

4. Components

  • Sampling Design: Selection of samples
  • Observational Design: Method of data collection
  • Statistical Design: Data analysis techniques
  • Operational Design: Execution plan and schedule

5. Types of Research Design

  • Exploratory: Used when problem is not clearly defined
  • Descriptive: Describes characteristics of population
  • Diagnostic: Identifies causes of problems
  • Hypothesis Testing: Tests hypotheses and cause-effect relationships
06

Important Concepts (Part 1)

Variables Measurable characteristics that can change.
- Independent: Cause
- Dependent: Effect
- Extraneous: Variables not part of study but affecting results.
Hypothesis Testable statement relating variables. Includes Null Hypothesis (H₀) and Alternative Hypothesis (H₁).
06 & 07

Concepts & Principles

Additional Concepts

  • Control: Minimizing effects of extraneous variables.
  • Confounded Relationship: Relationship affected by extraneous variables.
  • Experimental/Control Groups: Control Group → normal conditions; Experimental Group → receives treatment.
  • Treatments: Different conditions applied.
  • Experimental Units: Units on which experiments are conducted.

7. Basic Principles of Experimental Design

  • Replication: Repeating experiment multiple times (Improves reliability/accuracy).
  • Randomization: Assigning treatments randomly (Eliminates bias).
  • Local Control: Controlling known extraneous variables (Reduces variability).
